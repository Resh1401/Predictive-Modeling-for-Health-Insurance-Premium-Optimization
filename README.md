1.	Introduction

The healthcare industry plays a crucial role in society, as it directly influences individuals' well-being, quality of life and life expectancy. A fundamental aspect of healthcare is health insurance, which serves as a vital safety net, shielding individuals from the potentially crippling costs associated with medical care. Without adequate coverage, people may face financial hardship, especially when faced with serious illnesses or injuries. The high costs of treatments, medications and hospital stays can lead to long-term economic strain, further affecting their quality of life.

However, from the perspective of insurance companies, the challenge lies in balancing the provision of coverage with the management of financial risk. To remain financially viable, insurance providers must optimize their pricing models, which involves accurately assessing the risk associated with insuring an individual.

Traditionally, health insurance premiums are determined based on factors such as age, medical history, and current health status. However, lifestyle choices such as diet, exercise and smoking habits also have a significant impact on an individual’s health and the likelihood of requiring costly medical interventions. As a result, insurance companies are increasingly leveraging health and lifestyle data to refine their pricing strategies.

2. Objective

The primary objective of this project is to develop a predictive model that estimates the optimal insurance premium for an individual, considering a variety of health and lifestyle-related factors. By doing so, the project aims to achieve several key outcomes:

* Personalized and Equitable Pricing: The development of a predictive model tailored to individual risk profiles will enable insurance companies to provide premiums that reflect the true health risks associated with each person. This approach will lead to more personalized and fair pricing, rather than relying on broad demographic categories such as age or location.

* Incentivizing Healthier Lifestyles: By tying insurance premiums to lifestyle choices, the project aims to encourage individuals to adopt healthier behaviours. Offering financial incentives for maintaining a healthy lifestyle such as regular exercise, balanced diets, and avoiding risky habits like smoking can promote a culture of prevention and wellness.

* Financial Sustainability: The implementation of more accurate and data-driven pricing models has the potential to reduce overall claims and medical costs, benefiting insurance companies with long-term savings. This creates a more sustainable financial model for both individuals and insurers.

* Improved Public Health: By fostering a culture of wellness and preventive care, this approach can lead to better public health outcomes. When individuals are motivated to take charge of their health in ways that directly influence their insurance premiums, society as a whole can benefit from healthier, more informed citizens.

In essence, this project seeks to create a system that accurately predicts and optimizes health insurance costs, ensuring fairness and financial sustainability. By integrating personalized health data into the pricing model, it aims to develop a more equitable, efficient, and health-conscious insurance system that benefits both individuals and the broader healthcare industry.
